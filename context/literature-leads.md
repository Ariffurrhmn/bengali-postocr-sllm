## Should be cited (high priority)
- **Boros, E., et al. (2024). "Post-Correction of Historical Text Transcripts with Large Language Models: An Exploratory Study."** 14 foundation models, zero- and few-shot, several languages, periods and transcription qualities; abstract concludes LLMs are "anything but efficient" at this task. Closest prior negative result to ours; belongs in Sec II-C and VI-B. Venue to verify (likely the LaTeCH-CLfL 2024 workshop at EACL). https://consensus.app/papers/details/f03628243373535a8c00303966736b39/

## Worth citing (related work / discussion)
- **Bhandari, A., et al. (2026). "A Framework and Dataset for Contextual Post-OCR Correction." ACM TALLIP.** Fine-tuned pre-trained LM with sentence context for Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi OCR: CER 10.20→6.73%, 6.10→1.39%, 8.19→3.29%. The nearest Indic post-OCR work; supervised, modern print, low input CER. Sec II-E, and the "no comparable resource" sentence in II-B. https://consensus.app/papers/details/2d881d6b070a57b2ab7a1dbfebf7dc05/
- **Bourne, J. (2025). "Scrambled text: fine-tuning language models for OCR error correction using synthetic data." IJDAR.** Fine-tuning on synthetic character-level (Markov) corruption reduced CER 55% over the base LM and beat training on real data; under-corrupted training data works better than over-corrupted. Directly relevant to the M2 design (our noise levels 0.02-0.40) and its discussion. https://consensus.app/papers/details/29799409937c5b4d969b416b2275f724/
- **Santos, S., et al. (2024). "A proposal for post-OCR spelling correction using Language Models." LatinX in AI @ NeurIPS 2024.** Fine-tuned BART and ByT5 beat Llama 1/2 and SymSpell on Brazilian handwritten essays; ByT5 handled high noise well. Supports the choice of ByT5 for M2. https://consensus.app/papers/details/aed1a1689173510d85ec89ff98d108ef/
- **Santos de Araújo, S., et al. (2025). "A Proposal of Post-OCR Spelling Correction Using Monolingual Byte-level Language Models." ACM DocEng 2025.** Monolingual ByT5 variants reduce CER on IAM, RIMES, BRESSAY. https://consensus.app/papers/details/9681ec863dce5ed4afc13072afc1c710/
- **Greif, G., et al. (2025). "Multimodal LLMs for OCR, OCR Post-Correction, and Named Entity Recognition in Historical Documents." arXiv.** Multimodal post-correction (image + OCR text) of German city directories 1754-1870 reaches <1% CER. Relevant to "experiments not performed" (iii), multimodal transcription. https://consensus.app/papers/details/a5c30cecabb95621834a9c89819fb2fd/
- **Bourne, J. (2024). "CLOCR-C: Context Leveraging OCR Correction with Pre-trained Language Models." arXiv.** Seven LMs on 19th-century serials; best >60% CER reduction; socio-cultural context in the prompt helps. https://consensus.app/papers/details/d9e973b908385feeb53e8e4e90a003cd/

## Decoding pitfall (lead contribution): novelty check
- One search (repetition penalty, input tokens, copying, correction) found **no paper making our point** that the penalty and n-gram ban act on prompt tokens in decoder-only models and distort the evaluation of correction. Papers on repetition (Xu et al. 2022 "Learning to Break the Loop"; Yao et al. 2025 "Repeat Curse"; Ginart et al. 2025 "LZ Penalty") treat loops in open-ended generation.
- Closest: **Huang, D.-H., et al. (2025). "RAP: A Metric for Balancing Repetition and Performance in Open-Source Large Language Models."** Excessive repetition penalty compromises quality on QA and machine translation; tunes the penalty per task. Could be cited in Sec III-D as evidence that the penalty costs task quality, while our point (its scope includes the prompt) stays ours. https://consensus.app/papers/details/fc3ce0744b105a2884024be6ba4d72b0/
- One search is not proof of absence; the stuck research thread should be read when it finishes. Keep the paper's wording as "we show", not "we are the first".
